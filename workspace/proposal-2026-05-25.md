## 5. Known weaknesses

I've identified three honest flaws in this proposal that the Risk Officer should challenge:

1. **Selling into weakness on the losers:** BAJAJHFL (-45%), PRAJIND (-42%), RVNL (-38%), IREDA (-29%) are all deeply underwater. Exiting now crystallizes ₹34K in losses (₹8K + ₹6K + ₹6K + ₹13K). If any of these positions rally 20-30% in the next month (not impossible—RVNL RSI is at 27, technically oversold), we'll have sold at the bottom. Counterargument: 13-month horizon doesn't give us time to wait for multi-year turnarounds, and capital preservation (Hard Rule #6) trumps speculation on oversold bounces. But the timing risk is real.

2. **Profit-booking at market peaks—timing risk:** BSE and CUMMINSIND exits happen while the broader market is in a rally (NIFTY +1.55% last week, Bank Nifty +1.78% today, VIX down to 17). Technically we're selling strength, not weakness, but if this rally extends another 10-15% over the next 2 months, we'll have left money on the table. The valuation argument (BSE PE 81, CUMMINSIND flagged EXIT) supports the exit, but market timing is notoriously hard. If Risk wants to stage these exits (e.g., sell half today, half in a week), that's defensible.

3. **Liquidity buffer restored but equity still at 59%—is that enough de-risking?** This rebalance drops equity from 70% to 59%, but with 13 months to goal, the user_plan.md says "corpus must be substantially capital-safe by Q2 2027." At 59% equity, we're not even halfway to capital-safe yet. A -15% NIFTY drawdown (not extreme in a correction) would still cost ₹1.33L, eating 5.3% of the goal corpus. Risk Officer might argue we should trim more aggressively now (target 50% equity) rather than stage it over 2-3 sessions. My defense: a single-day 11-percentage-point equity drop is already material; doing 20 percentage points in one go creates execution risk and might trigger panic if the market rallies after we exit. But the criticism is valid—we're still equity-heavy for the horizon.
