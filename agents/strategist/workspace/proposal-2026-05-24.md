# Initial Allocation Proposal — 2026-05-24

## 💡 In plain English

I'm proposing you invest ₹3.5 lakh from your ten-lakh fixed deposit today, spread across four well-diversified funds. The breakdown: ₹2 lakh goes into two broad stock-market index funds (₹1.25L in the main Nifty 50 tracker and ₹75K in a mid-cap index fund), ₹1 lakh into a balanced fund that mixes stocks and bonds, and ₹50K into a liquid debt fund as a safety cushion. This leaves ₹6.5 lakh still in your FD. Starting next month, we'll add ₹60,000 monthly through automatic investments (SIPs) across the same four funds.

**Why this approach:** Your thirty-year timeline gives you room to benefit from stock-market growth, but your low-risk comfort level means we can't rush. This first step puts only 20% of your money into pure stocks (the two index funds), and even that's split between large and mid-sized companies. The balanced fund (₹1L) and debt fund (₹50K) act as stabilizers. By starting small and adding monthly, we avoid the danger of putting everything in at once during a shaky market — today's fear gauge (VIX at 17.91) and mixed price action suggest caution is smart.

**Plan B if you'd rather wait:** If this feels too aggressive given market uncertainty, we can cut the first tranche to ₹2 lakh total (₹1L index, ₹50K balanced, ₹50K debt fund only), skip the mid-cap portion entirely, and re-evaluate next month. You'd still have ₹8 lakh in the FD and meet all safety rules.

## 6. Risk Officer — Questions You Should Challenge Me On

1. **Market timing risk:** VIX at 17.91 and NIFTY down -2.70% over the last month suggest we're not in a calm, bullish environment. Am I deploying ₹3.5L at a local top? What if the market drops another 10% in the next two months?

2. **Mid-cap allocation in first tranche:** I'm proposing ₹75K into mid-caps immediately. Mid-caps typically fall 10-15 percentage points more than large-caps in downturns. Given the user's low-risk profile, should we defer mid-cap exposure to Month 2 or 3 after observing market stability?

3. **Balanced fund opacity:** I'm recommending ₹1L into a Balanced Advantage Fund, but these funds use tactical allocation models that aren't fully transparent. If the fund manager makes poor equity-debt shift decisions, the "automatic rebalancing" benefit could backfire. Should we use a simpler 60-40 hybrid fund with a static mandate instead?

4. **SIP front-loading assumption:** The ₹60K/month SIP assumes the user will commit for 10-11 months without interruption. If the user's income situation changes (job loss, medical emergency), we're locking capital into funds with exit loads. Have we stress-tested the SIP commitment against the user's income stability?

5. **Opportunity cost of 65% FD holding:** By keeping ₹6.5L in FD (likely earning 6-7% annually), I'm prioritizing safety over growth. If the market enters a multi-year bull run, this conservative stance could cost significant compounding. Am I being *too* cautious given the 30-year horizon?
