## 4. Adversarial Concerns

1. **Mid-cap first-tranche allocation in uncertain market:** The proposal includes ₹75K (7.5%) in Nifty Midcap 150 Index Fund in the very first deployment, despite VIX at 17.91 and weak recent market breadth (11 declining / 8 advancing in top 20 stocks). Mid-caps historically amplify drawdowns by 10-15 percentage points versus large-caps during corrections. For a low-risk user making their first equity purchase, is it prudent to include mid-cap exposure *today* rather than deferring it to Month 2-3 after observing market stability? The Strategist acknowledges this risk in Section 6.2 but proceeds anyway. If NIFTY corrects 10% in the next 60 days, the mid-cap tranche could see a 15-20% drawdown, testing the user's actual risk tolerance versus stated tolerance.

2. **Balanced Advantage Fund opacity and tactical risk:** ₹1L (10% of corpus) is allocated to a Balanced Advantage Fund, described as having "automatic tactical shifts." These funds use proprietary models to adjust equity-debt ratios, which means the user is outsourcing market-timing decisions to a fund manager whose skill and process are opaque. If the fund manager makes poor tactical calls (e.g., shifting to 80% equity at a market peak, or 20% equity during a rally), the "auto-rebalancing" benefit turns into a drag. The Strategist flags this in Section 6.3 but doesn't quantify how to evaluate whether a given fund's tactical record justifies the allocation. Should we require the Execution Agent to screen for funds with 5+ year track records of downside capture <90% before purchase?

3. **SIP commitment rigidity without income stress-test:** The proposal assumes ₹60K/month SIPs will continue uninterrupted for 10-11 months. But the user's monthly surplus is ₹75K (₹1L income - ₹25K outflows), leaving only ₹15K/month buffer. If the user faces a 20% income drop (job change, pay cut, business slowdown), their surplus shrinks to ₹55K/month, forcing them to either: (a) break SIPs (triggering behavioral/exit-load costs), or (b) dip into the FD buffer faster than planned. The Strategist mentions this in Section 6.4 but doesn't model what happens if income volatility hits. Should we require a 3-month SIP pause clause or a flexible SIP step-down plan (e.g., ₹60K → ₹40K → ₹20K) if income drops?

4. **Opportunity cost vs. safety trade-off is tilted too conservatively for a 30-year horizon:** The 65% FD allocation (₹6.5L earning ~6-7% annually) is extremely safe, but over 30 years, the compounding differential between 7% FD returns and 10-12% equity returns is massive. At 7%, ₹6.5L grows to ₹49.5L in 30 years. At 10%, it grows to ₹1.13Cr. That's a ₹63.5L opportunity cost for holding excess safety capital. The Strategist acknowledges this in Section 6.5 ("Am I being *too* cautious?") but doesn't propose a mechanism to dynamically shift FD→equity as the portfolio grows or the user's confidence increases. Should we add a review trigger: "If portfolio exceeds ₹15L by Month 12 and user reports comfort with volatility, propose accelerating equity allocation to 40-45%"?

5. **Manual stress test lacks Monte Carlo rigor:** The stress test in Section 5 uses two historical scenarios (-55% equity crash, -35% shock) but applies flat percentage losses without modeling path-dependency, correlation shifts, or volatility clustering. For example, in 2008, mid-caps didn't fall uniformly by -65%; some fell -80%, others -50%, and the drawdown took 18 months with multiple false rallies. A manual calculation can't capture this. The Strategist notes "recovery_sim.py requires existing holdings" as the reason for skipping it, but we could run a *forward-looking* Monte Carlo sim on the *proposed* holdings by mocking a holdings.json file with the post-deployment positions. Should we require that before approval, or accept manual stress-testing for initial allocations?
